Live Photo editing FAQ: twelve questions, answered plainly

Editing a Live Photo is not like editing a photograph, because a Live Photo is two things at once: a still image and a short clip with sound. Most questions people ask come down to that fact — whether the motion survives an edit, whether the original is safe, and where the result can be shared without being flattened. Below are twelve straight answers, using Filmine, a Live Photo darkroom for iPhone, as the worked example.

1. What exactly is a Live Photo?

When Live Photos is switched on, your iPhone records about a second and a half either side of the shutter press. You get a full-resolution still — the key frame — plus a short video with audio, linked together in your library. Long-press the thumbnail and the clip plays. It is one item in Photos, but two assets underneath, which is why so many editors mishandle it.

2. Will it still move after I edit it?

With Filmine, yes. The grade is applied to the key still and to every frame of the clip, and the app writes out a new Live Photo with the motion and the sound intact. Elsewhere, it depends: many editors accept a Live Photo and save a flat image, and some apps save a video file instead, which moves but no longer behaves like a Live Photo. The safe test is to long-press the result in Photos and look for the LIVE badge.

3. Does editing change my original photo?

No. Filmine always saves a new Live Photo alongside the one you started with. The original is read, never written to, so if you dislike the result you can simply delete the new copy. This is also why it is worth experimenting freely.

4. Why does exporting take a few seconds?

Because there is real work to do. A single filtered photograph is one frame of processing; a Live Photo is the still plus every frame of the clip, all of which have to be graded identically so the colour does not jump when playback starts, then reassembled with the audio into a fresh Live Photo. On top of that, everything is computed on your iPhone rather than on a server. A few seconds is the honest cost of keeping the motion.

5. Why can't I select an ordinary photo in the app?

Filmine only handles Live Photos, so still images do not appear in its picker at all. That is deliberate rather than a missing feature — every part of the app, from the frame layers to the film stocks, is designed around a moving asset. If a picture you expected is not listed, it was probably captured with Live Photos switched off, or the motion was removed at some earlier point.

6. Can I post the result to Instagram, and will it still move?

It depends entirely on where it is going. Sharing to the system Photos library, sending by AirDrop, or sending through WeChat generally preserves the Live Photo, so the person at the other end can press and hold. Instagram does not treat Live Photos as a native format in the ordinary posting flow: you should expect it to become a still image, or to require you to save it out as a video first, in which case it plays but is no longer a Live Photo. Test with one post before assuming.

7. What about privacy — where do my photos go?

Nowhere. Filmine processes everything on the device. There is no server, so your photographs are never uploaded; there is no analytics and no data collection of any kind. The app reads the Live Photo you choose, does the work locally, and writes the result back to your library.

8. Do I need to create an account?

No. There is no sign-up, no login and no account. You open the app and use it.

9. Is it free, and where do I check the price?

The film stocks and the portrait presets are free. Frames are partly free: three of the ten options — None, Polara and Signature — are included at no cost, and the remaining eight are unlocked either by an annual subscription, which comes with a seven-day trial for new users, or by a one-time permanent purchase. Current pricing for your region is shown on the App Store listing and inside the app, which is the only place it will be accurate.

10. What can I actually adjust?

Less than you might expect, on purpose. There are eleven film stocks, each with exactly two sliders: Intensity and Texture. Grain is part of texture rather than a separate control, because a grain amount detached from the rest of the look tends to flicker in motion. There are five portrait presets with two sliders of their own, subject fill and background fade. And there are ten frames plus a no-frame option, with no parameters at all. The app does not do collage, free-form cropping, video trimming, custom text or AI generation.

11. What languages and what iOS version?

Filmine requires iOS 18 or later. The current list of supported languages is shown on the App Store listing, which is kept up to date as localisations are added — worth checking there rather than relying on a page like this one.

12. Is there an Android, iPad or Mac version?

No. Filmine is an iPhone app only. There is no Android version, no Mac version and no iPad version, and none is a workaround away — the app depends on the Live Photo format and on the way iOS presents it. If you have received a Live Photo on another platform, it will usually have arrived as a still or a video rather than as a Live Photo in the first place.

One habit worth forming

Whenever you try a new editor on a Live Photo, spend thirty seconds verifying the result: find it in Photos, check for the LIVE badge, press and hold, and watch whether the colour shifts at the moment playback begins. A shift means the cover frame was graded but the clip was not. A file that no longer moves cannot be repaired afterwards, because the video half is simply not in the exported copy — so verify early, and keep the original until you are satisfied.
